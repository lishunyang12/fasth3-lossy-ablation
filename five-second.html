03 / Measurement protocol

Each arm has two excluded warmup requests followed by five formal requests. Timing starts immediately before a local HTTP request and ends after all MP4 response bytes arrive. Model loading, warmup compilation, and post-response media validation are excluded. Encoding and transfers performed by each pipeline are included. Chat interpretation, streaming queues, RTMP upload, and Twitch playback delay are outside this boundary.

RTF is request latency divided by actual video duration; a value below one is faster than playback. GPU clocks were not locked, and arms were measured sequentially. Small median differences do not establish a statistically significant speedup. Every dot is one formal request. The dashed line marks 124 / 24 = 5.167 seconds. Use the per-arm playback column when a pipeline emits a different frame count. Warmup and loading times are available in the results JSON.

The unchanged prompt schedules its third shot at 6.6 seconds, beyond this five-second request. The model can compress the shot timing. These outputs compare performance and generation under identical input; they do not validate strict execution of the full three-shot script.

The optimized stack retains exact AdaLN caching, split QK/V overlap, MXFP8 SwiGLU fusion, tiled output projection, batched VAE decoding with gather overlap, fused output blending/RGB conversion, overlapped audio/video decoding, early AAC encoding, and chunked MP4 output. BF16 first-step weight prefetch is counted in request latency.

The first-step rollback retains pre-quantization student weights: 24 blocks stay on GPU and 26 use pinned CPU buffers with prefetch. Both optimized precision schedules still use an NVFP4 VAE. The audio decoder is unchanged, but DiT precision can affect jointly generated audio.

“Original” refers to the pinned FastVideo preview baseline used by the existing gallery (source 3196835; checkpoint revision b65818d), not every FastH3 release. Ulysses transports 38,168 rows in the original stack and 38,208 in native serving because their padding alignment differs. Original-source checks cover 1,284 files against the previously recorded hashes. The original full checkpoint and the optimized base-plus-adapter deployment are different artifacts; cross-stack differences also include sharding, residency, decoder precision, and output handling. Therefore a whole-stack timing difference is not an isolated quantization or PR speedup. Within each PR pair, those choices are fixed. The emitted H.264 bitstreams report CRF 18 for native serving and CRF 23 for stock FastVideo, so cross-stack visual comparisons also include different encoding settings; neither is lossless video encoding. One prompt and seed cannot establish a general quality ranking.

Prompt — English display translation

The two Mandarin dialogue lines below are translated for this English page only. Every benchmark uses the verbatim original prompt, including its original Mandarin dialogue and timecodes.

integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Cinematic live-action fantasy inside a small wooden teahouse suspended beneath a colossal moon, shown in a symmetrical wide shot through open paper doors. An elderly tea master in an indigo robe, with silver hair pinned high and a warm gravelly Mandarin voice (S1), pours glowing tea into two porcelain cups while a young female radio astronomer in a cream flight suit, with a clear fast Mandarin voice (S2), adjusts a brass receiver beside her. The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed as lunar dust drifts upward instead of falling, and the tea master says: <d>[Chinese] The moon is not silent; it is only waiting for us to hear it.</d> [Shot 2] At 00:03.400, the camera cuts to a macro close-up of the tea surface. Concentric ripples become a precise star map, tiny points of light connect across the cup, and the receiver needle snaps toward a distant signal. The astronomer leans into the eyepiece, her face illuminated by moving constellations, and replies: <d>[Chinese] Signal locked.</d> [Shot 3] At 00:06.600, the shot cuts outside to a sweeping arc around the teahouse as the vast moon slowly opens into layered mechanical petals, revealing a luminous blue core. The two women step onto the balcony without changing clothes or positions relative to each other; the tea master raises her cup while the astronomer sends one narrow beam from the receiver into the core. The camera pulls out with large amplitude at slow speed until the teahouse becomes a warm point against the unfolding lunar structure.
overall_soundscape: Porcelain touches wood with delicate clicks above the soft hiss of boiling water and the papery flutter of the doors. A low receiver hum gains harmonics as the signal locks, followed by distant metal plates shifting across the moon and a soft pressure wave moving through airborne dust.
non_diegetic_music: Slow guzheng harmonics over sustained glassy synthesizer tones at 68 BPM. A restrained taiko pulse enters when the lunar petals open, then resolves into one long bowed-cymbal shimmer.
